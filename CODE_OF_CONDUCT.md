# Code of Conduct

These are small tools maintained by one person. The bar is simple and it is not
negotiable.

## What is expected

Be straightforward and be kind. Assume the person on the other end is acting in
good faith and is short on time. Criticise the code, the claim, or the design —
never the person making it.

Technical disagreement is welcome and is most of the point. "This is wrong, and
here is the case that shows it" is a contribution. So is "I could not get this
to run." So is "the docs say X and the code does Y."

## What is not

Harassment, personal attacks, demeaning or discriminatory comments, sustained
disruption, publishing anyone's private information, or unwelcome attention of
any kind. Discrimination on the basis of anything a person did not choose is
out, whether or not it is dressed up as a joke.

None of this depends on whether the target is a maintainer, a contributor, or
someone passing through.

## Scope

This applies in issues, pull requests, discussions, commit messages, and any
other space these projects occupy — and to public conduct elsewhere when it
plainly reflects on the project.

## Reporting

Report to **dev@swamplink.com**. Reports go to the maintainer directly and stay
private. Include what happened, where, and links if you have them.

There is no committee and no process theatre. What you will get is a reply, a
decision, and — where the conduct warrants it — a comment removed, an
interaction limited, or a participant blocked. What you will not get is a
demand that you justify being uncomfortable.

Reports about the maintainer's own conduct go to the same address, and the
honest limitation is worth stating plainly: **on a solo project there is nobody
above the maintainer to appeal to.** If that is not good enough for your
situation, it is a reasonable reason to fork rather than to engage, and no
hard feelings.
